# Evidence Hierarchy for Web3 Impact Claims

## Evidence that can support on-chain activity

An on-chain record may support factual statements about:

- A transaction or contract interaction occurring.
- The timestamp, network, transaction hash, asset, amount, and addresses involved.
- The existence of a deployed contract or attestation record.
- The execution of encoded program logic, subject to correct contract interpretation.

An on-chain record does not independently prove:

- The real-world identity of a wallet holder.
- The beneficiary's identity, consent, need, or receipt of value.
- Goods delivery or service completion.
- The truth of an oracle input, uploaded document, or attestation content.
- Measured environmental or social outcome.
- Causality, additionality, permanence, absence of leakage, or lack of harm.
- Legal compliance, tax treatment, charity status, or responsible use of funds.

## Evidence strength indicators

Higher-strength evidence tends to include:

- Independent third-party verification.
- Primary records with provenance and timestamps.
- Clear methodology, sample, baseline, denominator, and measurement criteria.
- Evidence that connects disbursement to delivery and measured outcomes.
- Reconciliation between financial flows, implementation records, and outcome measurement.
- Disclosure of limitations, conflicts, missing data, and uncertainty.

## Common overclaiming patterns

Avoid these unsupported leaps:

- "Funds sent on-chain" becomes "beneficiaries received support."
- "NFT or attestation minted" becomes "impact was verified."
- "Tokens distributed" becomes "users experienced improved outcomes."
- "A carbon-related asset was purchased" becomes "carbon was removed or emissions were reduced."
- "A dashboard metric increased" becomes "the program caused societal improvement."
