# Privacy and Ethics

## Data minimization

Collect and publish only what is necessary for the stated verification purpose.

Do not publish:

- Seed phrases, private keys, API keys, access tokens, passwords, or signing material.
- Government IDs, full names, home addresses, private phone numbers, or personal financial records.
- Exact locations of vulnerable individuals or communities when disclosure could create harm.
- Raw health, education, legal, immigration, or safeguarding records.

## Consent and beneficiary protection

Before collecting or publishing beneficiary-linked evidence:

- Establish whether meaningful, informed consent is possible and appropriate.
- Explain intended use, retention, access, and publication risks.
- Avoid making support conditional on public disclosure when that creates pressure or harm.
- Use aggregation and pseudonymization where possible.
- Consider whether an immutable public chain is appropriate for the data.

## Independence and conflicts

Disclose:

- Whether the project generated, selected, or paid for the evidence.
- Any financial relationship between verifier and project.
- Data gaps, exclusions, sampling limitations, and methodology changes.
- Whether external review was independent and what it actually covered.
